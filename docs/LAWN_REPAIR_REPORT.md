# Prologue grass, lawns & ground — repair report

Branch `claude/prologue-grass-lawn-ground-repair`, continued from the vegetation benchmark at
`f1f085a9f448c22ecb8a4fa3eed967a2e7603428` (kept as it was for comparison and recovery). Nothing is merged into `main`.
Without `?vegetation` the game is unchanged.

**Evidence and its limits.** In-game captures are the shipped renderer under **software WebGL** (Chromium + ANGLE
SwiftShader, **not a GPU**), from the game's own riding and walking cameras placed by the QA hook. They are review
cameras, not a person's playthrough. The Blender renders are offline Cycles inspection renders of the assets (**not the
game**), labelled as such. Every performance number is a software-renderer or file number. No real-GPU frame time has
been measured.

## 1. Why the lawns looked like a green slab with clumps on it

| Cause | In the first benchmark (B) |
|---|---|
| The ground was not grass | The lawn material was one flat colour (the street palette's olive). Large noise patches 7–20 m across sat on it, plus ambient.js's own broad dry tint (patches tens of metres across). Nothing at the scale of blades, and no normals. Up close and at grazing angles it was a smooth painted plane. |
| The 3D grass was separate objects | About 3,000 tufts of big blades (`grass.lawn.*` / `grass.long.*`, 84–132 triangles each), each a little bush, scattered on a 0.36 m grid with random sizes. A tuft and the gap around it were different things, so every tuft read as an island. |
| Two palettes | The tufts had their own atlas colours (brighter, yellower) and their own shading. Nothing tied them to the ground beneath them. |
| A visible edge | Tufts became crossed cards beyond 10 m and vanished at 22 m into the flat colour. Past that, the lawn was the slab. |
| The lookout | The same flat ground with ~12,500 meadow cards and tufts scattered on it. The worn patch under the swing was a dark decal. |

## 2. What changed (C)

**The ground itself is grass** (`dist/vegetation-lawn.js`, `patchGround`). The lawn materials of streets.js
(`world.grassMats`) are drawn as a world-space grass surface:

- **Authored top-down blade textures** (`veg.lawn.glb`, generated by `tools/blender/vegetation/lawn_kit.py`, original
  work):
  - mown lawn: 0.5 m tile at 1024 px, 0.49 mm per pixel, ~26,000 blades in five depth layers, with dark depth between
    blades, cut tips and a little thatch;
  - unmown meadow: 1 m tile;
  - trodden soil;
  - white clover.

  Colour and height are in one RGBA image; normal maps come from the height.
- **No visible tiling.** The lawn texture is sampled at two scales, 0.5 m and 1.37 m (the second turned 37°), and the two
  are blended by height (whichever blade stands higher wins, biased by a slow noise), so neither period shows. Fine
  mottling sits on top. Anisotropic filtering is set to the device maximum.
- **Restrained broad variation.** Brightness varies ±3.5 % and hue a few percent. The large blotches of the first
  benchmark and ambient.js's broad dry tint are replaced, not added to.
- **Per-lot care.** Each house lot runs halfway to its neighbours on its side, and each gets one care level:
  - **kept** (38 %): shorter, even and greener; faint mowing stripes on most, whose brightness depends on the view
    direction as real stripes do;
  - **average** (44 %): clover patches, a few thin spots;
  - **rough** (18 %): longer, lighter, yellower grass; small dry and thin spots, a few weeds.

  The curb strip belongs to its lot.
- **The lookout.** The knoll uses the meadow texture. The trodden way from the stop is flattened, lighter grass with bare
  earth only down its middle. The swing's patch is bare earth with ragged edges, replacing the decal. There is a worn
  place in front of the bench, a root zone of earth at the trunk, the oak's shade (darker, thinner grass), and flattened
  grass where the old bike lies.

**Continuous blades grow from it** (`BladeField`):
- **Tile.** One 1 m × 1 m tile of 777 fine blades in 380 shoots of 1–3 blades. Blades are about 4–6 mm wide as drawn and
  4–10 cm tall on lawns, slightly curved and leaning, 3 triangles each.
- **Placement.** The tile is instanced on every lawn cell of a world-aligned 1 m grid within 13 m of the camera. Each cell
  is turned a quarter turn and/or mirrored, and sheared onto the ground's plane.
- **One material.** Each blade takes its colour from the ground under its own root, through the same GLSL function as
  the ground (`lawnPlace`): shaded at the root, lit at the tip. Blades and ground are therefore one palette. Blade normals
  lean to the ground's, so both take the light the same way.
- **Thinning without a ring.** Blades are ordered by a blue-noise rank, so every prefix of the tile is evenly spread.
  With distance, the share of standing blades falls smoothly (all within 2.5 m, about a third at 6 m, about a tenth at
  9 m, none at 13 m), and each blade shrinks to nothing as the share passes its rank. There is no ring, no line and no
  popping.
- **Free LODs.** A tile's sparser LOD is a prefix of the same index buffer (2,331 / 792 / 318 triangles). The CPU switches
  a tile only where the blades it drops have already faded, so the switch is invisible by construction.
- **Clean, natural edges.** Edge cells carry an 8 × 8 mask (12.5 cm). Blades stop at walks, curbs, drives, porches, beds,
  posts, fences, hydrants and trunks with a slightly ragged line, not a stamp. On rough lots the edge blades grow a
  little longer and lean over the edge, as unedged lawns do.
- **Lot heights.** Blade height follows the lot: kept lawns are shorter, rough lots longer. The lookout's knoll grows long
  grass 14–37 cm tall that thins in the oak's shade and on the paths.
- **Wind.** Blades run on the trees' wind uniforms (ambient.js's breeze and gusts), bending most at the tips. Kept lawns
  barely move; the long grass sways.
- **Cost control.** Lawn masks are built lazily in 16 m chunks within a 3.5 ms-per-frame budget, ahead of the rider. Tiles
  behind the camera are skipped (generous cone), and there are only 3 draw calls.

**The meadow cards** (C): inside the field fence the long blades replace the cards. Only accents stay (black-eyed
Susans, seed stalks, tall grass along the fence and the edges). The field beyond the fence keeps its tall meadow, a
little thinner.

**Weeds.** Sparse rosettes and seed stalks (existing `veg.ground` cards): about 1 per 20 m² on rough lots, 1 per 80 m²
on average lots, none on kept ones.

**Nothing else changed.** These are untouched:
- the oak, the 44 trees and the 18 shrubs (byte for byte);
- houses, bikes, roads and walks;
- collisions, story, dialogue, chapters, menus, audio, sky and lighting.

## 8. Known limitations

- **No real GPU.** Every frame and number here is software rendering (SwiftShader). The repair moves cost to the GPU:
  - **Vertex work:** up to ~260k blade triangles at the lookout and ~60–220k on the street, each blade vertex reading the
    lot and lookout maps and the ground's colour.
  - **Fragment work:** the lawn ground samples up to about ten textures per pixel (two lawn scales and their normals; the
    meadow, soil and clover only where they show; the two maps).

  Frame time on the reference hardware is the next measurement.
- **Memory.** The lawn textures add about 27 MB of GPU texture memory (uncompressed RGBA8 + mips estimate) and the
  maps about 4 MB. KTX2 would cut that several times. The lawn file is 3.7 MB.
- **Where the blades grow.** Blades grow on Oak Hollow Drive's lawns (curb strip, front, side and back yards within 40 m
  of the centre line), around the cul-de-sac and on the lookout's knoll. Side streets and the far yards get the new
  ground material without blades; from the street the blades would be gone by that distance anyway.
- **Teleports.** The lawn masks are built ahead of the rider within a per-frame budget. After a jump (a chapter start, a
  DEV scene, a QA `place`) the ground shows at once and the blades fill in over a fraction of a second. The tests build
  synchronously (`__vegetation.tick()`).
- **Mowing stripes** are deliberately faint, and their brightness depends on the view direction, so from some angles a
  striped lawn shows none.
- **Weeds** are the first benchmark's ground cards (4 triangles), kept sparse. A weed set of their own would be a later
  pass.
- **The field beyond the lookout's fence** keeps its tall meadow cards (seen from the knoll at a distance). Inside the
  fence the long blades and the ground carry the grass.
- **Under the DEV flag only.** The ground treatment applies to every lawn material of the town while `?vegetation` is on
  (the per-lot care map covers Oak Hollow Drive; elsewhere lawns are average). The default game is unchanged.
