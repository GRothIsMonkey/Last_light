# Aftermath I — human playtest checklist

Everything here is what the automated runs **cannot** judge. The input-driven tests show that every beat can be reached
and finished, in every branch they try (`AFTERMATH1_TEST_REPORT.md`); they say nothing about whether a scene lands.
Play with sound on, in desktop Chrome or Edge, on a real GPU. Allow 35–50 minutes from Chapter Eight's end card.

How to start: finish Chapter Eight and press **Aftermath I →** on its end card (best: it carries Chapter Eight's state),
or **DEV → Aftermath I → a scene**. Note the clock time at each scene change if you can: pacing is the first thing to
judge.

## Before you start

- [ ] The Chapter Eight end card shows **Aftermath I →**, and pressing it leads into a card (WHAT WAS LEFT · SATURDAY,
      AUGUST 27, 2011) and then the school.
- [ ] Frame rate is playable in the gym (the busiest scene: ~30 people) and on Jamie's lawn at dusk (candles).

## Act I — Who came home (the school, Aug 27)

- [ ] **The lot**: does arriving read as relief and exhaustion, not as a new level? Is putting your bike in the rack, and
      taking Sam's handlebars because he can't let go, understandable without help?
- [ ] **The gym**: does it look and behave like a mass shelter (cots, lists, the board, the TV, volunteers, people who
      look at you and then past you at the doors)? Too many people? Too few?
- [ ] **Jamie's mother**: is it distinct, painful and *not* melodramatic? Does Reyes kneeling to tell her feel right? Does
      the cap choice (give it / keep it safe for her) feel like yours?
- [ ] **Alex's parents**: does their week of waiting come through (his father hoping; his mother very straight)? Is it
      different from Jamie's mother's scene?
- [ ] **Sam's father** ("I left your fan on") and **your mother**: relief and devastation at once?
- [ ] Faces: do the grown-ups' crying/grief faces read as grief, or as masks? (The overlay is new and has only been seen
      in captures.)

## Act II — What they remember (Aug 27–29)

- [ ] **The nurse's room**: washing your hands (the water going grey) — meaningful or busywork? Sam explaining the shear
      pin and then the chain: does his voice (captions) sound like Sam?
- [ ] **Reyes's record**: is laying out the four things in your own order understandable? Does Reyes come across as a
      credible, careful witness, not a conspiracy mouthpiece?
- [ ] **The list**: is the memory gap clear (a line with no name; you can't reach the word; the notebook in your own
      writing)? Is writing JAMIE (hold F) affecting or awkward?

## Act III — The places they left (September)

- [ ] **Behind the fence**: do you recognise Chapter Eight's street (Summerfield, the Kowalskis', Sam's house, the
      Kesslers', the Hendersons', 1052, the trench, the oak at the end)? Does the quiet feel worse than a chase would have?
- [ ] Reyes's "stop": did you understand you had to stop? Did it feel imposed or natural?
- [ ] At the barricade: did you want to look at more than one thing? Could you find the oak (it is far: 124 m)?
- [ ] **The bikes**: the bell (twice) — does the memory work *without* reading as a ghost? Walking the bikes out with a
      flat tyre flapping: too long (≈140 m), or right?
- [ ] **Jamie's porch**: where did you put his bike, and why? Lily's drawing of Biscuit: funny and sad, or too sweet?
      Could you walk away from "Was he scared?" and did that feel honoured?
- [ ] **Alex's garage and room**: the bike back on its hooks; the Sunday print ("Always the one holding it"). Did you go
      up to his room? Did you feel you could leave?
- [ ] **Jamie's lawn at dusk**: does it feel like a real vigil (candles, neighbours telling stories, laughter that
      catches itself), not a cutscene? Did you find Sam at the curb? Did you know you could go home when you wanted?
- [ ] **Alex's pictures**: does his father's short speech land? Did you find the picture he is in (his shadow), and was
      telling his father worth it?
- [ ] **The chain**: is turning the pedal backwards while Sam holds the chain a good "childhood action"? Is Sam's anger
      believable, and does refusing your guilt feel like friendship, not a lecture? Do the streetlights coming on and
      "rule four" work?

## Act IV — Leaving Oak Hollow (October)

- [ ] **The ride up the street**: does time passing show in the world (leaves, the fence moved and shut, Jamie's bike
      still there)? Is Reyes's update restrained and credible?
- [ ] **The letter**: are your mother's reasons enough to make the move feel unavoidable rather than imposed?
- [ ] **Packing**: is choosing what goes in the box (and what stays with you) meaningful? Is anything missing that you
      expected to find?
- [ ] **Goodbye, Sam**: does Sam checking your chain and giving you his notebook feel like Sam? Rule five: did you say it?
      Does the moment at the lamp post — waiting for the other two, and nobody coming — land without a ghost?
- [ ] **The road out**: did you look back? Did you look at what you kept? Did you choose when to look ahead, or did the
      road run out first? Does the end card feel understated and final?

## Throughout

- [ ] Sound: the crying and breathing are synthesized (Chapter Eight's, used sparingly). **Do they sound wrong?** Note
      every cue that pulls you out. Murmur in the gym and on the lawn, the machinery on the street, the bell, the
      freewheel, the tape: plausible?
- [ ] Captions: any line too long to read before it goes? Any line that sounds written rather than said?
- [ ] Did you ever not know what to do next (objective), or get stuck, or walk somewhere you shouldn't be able to?
- [ ] Sam over the weeks: shock → explaining → anger → tired and practical. Does he change, or is he the same sad face
      for two months?
- [ ] Anything that contradicts Chapters One–Eight (who has what, who is where, what was said)?
