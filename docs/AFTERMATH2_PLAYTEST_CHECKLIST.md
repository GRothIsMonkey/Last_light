# Aftermath II, the 2026 coda and the credits — human playtest checklist

Everything here is what the automated runs **cannot** judge. The input-driven tests show that every beat can be reached
and finished in every branch they try ([AFTERMATH2_TEST_REPORT.md](AFTERMATH2_TEST_REPORT.md)); the captures show how
frames look in software WebGL. Neither says whether a scene lands. Play with sound on, in desktop Chrome or Edge, on a
real GPU. Allow 20–30 minutes from Aftermath I's end card to the end screen.

How to start: finish Aftermath I and press **Aftermath II →** on its end card (best: it carries your Aftermath I save),
or **DEV → Aftermath II → a scene** / **DEV → 2026 · Credits → a scene**. Note the clock time at each scene change:
pacing is the first thing to judge.

## Before you start

- [ ] Aftermath I's end card shows **Aftermath II →**; pressing it gives a black card (AFTERMATH II / TEN YEARS LATER),
      then the car.
- [ ] Frame rate in the car on the drive in, and at the end of the street with the two kids riding (the busiest moments).

## The return (the drive, the street, Sam)

- [ ] **The drive**: is holding W to drive obvious? Does the title over the road (OAK HOLLOW — AUGUST 2021) feel
      restrained, not like a level banner? Does the street read as the same street, ten years on?
- [ ] **The car's cabin**: is it convincing enough from the driver's seat (wheel, dash, phone on the vent, the seat
      beside you)? Do your hands sit on the wheel?
- [ ] **Your house** (blue now, somebody's kid's scooter and hopscotch): does it hurt a little, and no more?
- [ ] **Sam at twenty-two** (provisional face): does he read as Sam grown up? Does "You actually came." land? Does the
      hello remember your goodbye from Aftermath I (hug / "See you, Sam." / the punch)?
- [ ] Is Sam easy to walk with (beside you, never in your way, never left behind, never stuck)?

## The old places

- [ ] **The hill**, **the corner where Alex turned off** (the bell remembered, not heard), **his house**, **the Sunday
      print**: affectionate, not maudlin?
- [ ] **The walk back to Jamie's**: does it feel long or empty? (Sam asks if you still ride; a basketball rolls into the
      gutter — did you notice you could throw it back?)
- [ ] **Jamie's house, somebody else's** (a car, a wreath, his mother's chair, a kid's bike): is Jamie remembered as
      funny (Biscuit), and is the grief quiet? If you kept his cap, does taking it out feel like yours to do?
- [ ] **Summerfield and the new houses**: do they read as rebuilt, not reset? Is anything confusing about where the
      street went down?
- [ ] **The streetlight**: sitting on the curb with him; his mom; "Turn the pedal backwards. Slow."; **the notebook**
      (Q/E pages): can you read ALEX · JAMIE · SAM and RULE 6. WRITE BACK.? Do the captions get in the way?
- [ ] Mrs. Pruitt's line: does it read as a fond story about Jamie, not as a joke at his expense?

## The oak and the end of the day

- [ ] The oak in the golden light: is "It's bigger." right? Do the optional looks (trunk, carving, roots, swing, the
      street) feel like your choice?
- [ ] **The two kids**: do they read as ordinary 2021 kids, never as echoes of the four of you? Is "See you tomorrow!"
      clearly heard/seen, and do the three lines after it (We used to do that. / Yeah. / Feels like it was yesterday.)
      land with their pause?
- [ ] The streetlights coming on; "Rule four."; looking back; "You coming?": is the ending of Aftermath II clear and
      unhurried?
- [ ] Nothing supernatural anywhere: no eyes, no sounds that suggest it, nothing in the trees. Confirm.

## The coda

- [ ] **One more lap**: for the first seconds, does it feel like the game has gone back to the Prologue?
- [ ] **The pullback**: is the first moment truly seamless (no jump, no flash, no change in colour or brightness)? Is it
      slow enough to be noticed gradually, and not so slow it drags? (Watch on a GPU: the captures are software.)
- [ ] Does the room reveal, without words, that he made the game and that it all happened (not a dream)? Read the cards
      and the map on the wall.
- [ ] The desk: the notebook, the photo-booth strip, his cap only if you kept it. Never Alex's camera.
- [ ] "I still remember.": does it land as a caption, at the photograph?
- [ ] LAST LIGHT on the monitor, the melody.

## The credits and after

- [ ] Readable at your window size; slow enough; the music quiet under it.
- [ ] The dedication alone, after the roll has gone: "For Jamie and Alex." / "And for every summer we thought would
      last forever."
- [ ] Skip (Esc / Space / Enter / the button) works, but never skips the dedication unseen.
- [ ] The end screen: THE END / Last Light / Summer, 2011.; Watch the credits again; Start over; Back to the title.
- [ ] Back on the title: Continue, the DEV menu and every earlier chapter still start.
- [ ] Nothing after the credits.

## Overall

- [ ] Emotional pacing across the whole of Aftermath II: where did it drag, where did it rush? (Note the times.)
- [ ] Does Sam feel like a real adult, the same friend grown up?
- [ ] Does Oak Hollow feel genuinely rebuilt, and did you recognize the important places without being told?
- [ ] Does the coda's reveal feel earned, not like a twist?
- [ ] Does the full ending, from "See you tomorrow!" to the dedication, feel satisfying?

## Sound (never listened to by the tests)

- [ ] The car's engine, the mower, the dog, the dribble and the rim, the kids' bell, the sprinkler, the lamps' click.
- [ ] The room's hush as the monitor recedes (the game's sound narrowing to two small speakers); the melody under the
      title and the credits. Too loud? Too quiet?
