# Story overview

## Premise

**Sunday, August 21, 2011.** Four friends (the police radio says Alex is 12; the others' ages are not yet verified)
ride their bikes through a suburban neighborhood at sunset, the end of summer. One by one they go home. Alex
goes first, down Briarwood Lane, ringing his bell twice. He never gets home. Over the next four days the other three —
**Jamie**, **Sam** and **you** — follow what the adults will not take seriously: a bicycle bell that rings where nobody
is, an old green bicycle that keeps appearing where Alex went, a figure that looks and sounds like Alex but is not, a
pale creature in the storm drain that is itself afraid of something, and a darkness that takes the lights, watches
through screens, copies what the boys do, and finally copies one of them.

The story is framed by an adult looking back ("You moved away in October. You meant to keep in touch."), and the
prologue ends on the line that defines the game: **"I thought I remembered everyone."**

## Tone

Nostalgic summer-2011 suburbia that turns, chapter by chapter, into horror. The friendship is the emotional core: the
dialogue is funny, specific and kind (the sock, Gary the shoe, "I brought batteries for the batteries"), so that the fear
lands on people the player likes. From 2026-10-23 the human developer's direction is explicit: this is a **genuine
horror game** (see [Horror design bible](../05_HORROR_DESIGN/HORROR_DESIGN_BIBLE.md)).

## The core mysteries

1. Where is Alex?
2. What rings the bell?
3. What is the old bike, and why does it go where Alex goes?
4. Who or what is "AR", and who is the forgotten fifth rider?
5. What wears Alex's (and then Jamie's) face?
6. What is the creature, and what is it afraid of?
7. What is the darkness that takes the lights (the Presence)?
8. What happened in 1966 and 1991, around the same dates?

All are ❓ UNKNOWN by design. Tracking: [Mystery and clue tracker](MYSTERY_AND_CLUE_TRACKER.md),
[Unanswered questions](UNANSWERED_QUESTIONS.md).

## What the story has never done (so far)

- Named the Presence or the creature on screen.
- Explained anything supernatural.
- Let anything touch or hurt the boys. There is no game over.
- Turned the player's head for them during play (except a few documented time fallbacks).
- Made the adults believe them.
