# Horror pacing

## The ladder

Every chapter so far climbs the same ladder: **ordinary → curious → uneasy → isolated → dread → seen → it comes → panic
→ escape → shaky relief → a quiet, wrong final image.** Chapter Three's rebuild stated it as: "normal investigation →
curiosity → unease → isolation → dread → 'someone has been down here' → 'why is *that* bicycle here?' → 'there is a
person down there' → 'was that Alex?' → something is in the tunnel with us → panic → escape → relief → fear of what they
actually saw."

## Pacing rules

1. **Shorten what drags.** The Chapter Three day was cut by 45%; the Chapter Five campfire's three-hour lapse was removed.
   Chapter Five's post-climax walk is the current problem.
2. **Quiet before loud.** Each peak is preceded by stillness or a wait the player can feel.
3. **After the peak, get out.** Relief should arrive within a minute or so of the climax; don't make the player walk an
   empty road back (Chapter Five lesson; Astra's redesign replaces "190–300 metres of empty recovery walking" with an
   announced passage of time — 🟡 in progress).
4. **Fallbacks keep momentum but never skip the payoff.** Hints → plainer objective → a friend leading → (rarely) a fade.
   Typical numbers: Chapter Four's hints at 6–12 s, nudges at 95–130 s, carries at 80–140 s; Chapter Five's Owl lead at
   120 s and fade at 240 s (first draft), the porch at 100/170 s, the double "never skipped on a timer".
5. **Friendship beats are pacing tools.** The dock, the stones, the campfire: optional or short, and they move on with a
   line if ignored.
6. **Each chapter ends on an image, not an explanation**: the bell (Ch1), "He heard it before he left." (Ch2), "Then what did
   we follow?" (Ch3), a streetlight blinking (Ch4), one light across the water (Ch5).
