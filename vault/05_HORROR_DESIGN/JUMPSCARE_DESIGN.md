# Jumpscare design

> The human developer wants **meaningful jumpscares** (2026-10-23). Earlier design notes said "no random jump scares"
> (Chapter Five first draft). These agree: a jumpscare is wanted when it is **caused**, **perceivable** and **earned**;
> a random noise or a face for no reason is not.

## Definition used in this vault

A **meaningful jumpscare** is a sudden, startling event that:

1. comes from something **in the world** that the player can see or clearly hear (and that Jamie and Sam react to);
2. **pays off** a build the player has been feeling (silence, a wait, a voice, a light);
3. **changes the situation** (now we run; now the way is blocked; now it is behind us);
4. happens **without seizing the camera** (it is placed where the player is likely to look, or the cues make them look).

## Rules

1. **One big startle per beat**, then let it breathe or turn it into action. Do not stack them.
2. **Build first**: silence (ambient dropout), a long wait (Chapter Three waited 6+ s before the close bell), or a cue.
3. **Make the companions sell it**: recoil, lights snapping round, a shout ("RUN!").
4. **Put it where the player will look**: in the beam, at the end of a corridor, at a door they are facing, or behind them
   after something made them turn. Use cue ladders, never a forced camera.
5. **Audio matters but is placeholder.** Every scare must work muted (sound will add to it later). See
   [Sound and silence](SOUND_AND_SILENCE.md).
6. **Do not repeat the same trick in the same way.** Chapter Five's lights come on *all at once* precisely so they do not
   repeat Chapter Four's line of streetlights.
7. **Record it** in the [Horror mechanics registry](HORROR_MECHANICS_REGISTRY.md).
